# Sources and licences

## Sources
- **Vasile Baltac**, *MECIPT – Mașina Electronică de Calcul a Institutului Politehnic din Timișoara – Evocări și Documente* (Recollections and Documents), 2011 — facsimiles 3 (the sine table), 4 (reprinting, 6 Sept 1962), 5 (INEX-1), 8 (the drum), 11 (ferrites). The facsimiles are not reproduced here.
- **Vasile Baltac**, MECIPT-1 laboratory work sheets, 1965–1966 — the surviving pages of sheets 7 and 8.
- **A. Geier**, “Programarea calculatorului electronic cifric MECIPT I în regimul virgulă mobilă” (Programming the MECIPT I in floating point), *Scientific and Technical Bulletin of the Polytechnic Institute of Timișoara*, 1961 — the instruction table, via the Romanian Wikipedia.
- **Romanian Wikipedia**, article [MECIPT](https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/MECIPT).
- **Dan D. Farcaș**, *MECIPT, o istorie subiectivă* (MECIPT, a subjective history), 2005.
- **Ștefan Mărușter**, *Povestea unei lucrări* (The story of a piece of work).
- **Vasile Baltac**, *Key Aspects of the History of Computing in Romania*.
- **marius0198**, 3D model [“MECIPT-1”](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/mecipt-1-62ddfc49f1c54967967d76abab5f3d2d), Sketchfab, 2020, [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) — panel textures and lamp positions, plus the 3D view. Modified by us: side panel textures converted to JPEG, the flash reflection removed from the right panel texture; when shown, the shelf and frame face painted in the model’s blue, the metal made glossy, the upper voltmeter needles re-seated, the missing voltmeter completed, the levers tilted with the switch state.
- **Turbojet**, [“MECIPT-1 Control desk”](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:MECIPT-1_Control_desk.jpg), Wikimedia Commons, 2011, CC BY-SA 4.0 — the look and colours of the restored console.
- **Turbojet**, [“MECIPT-1 Drum memory”](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:MECIPT-1_Drum_memory.jpg) and [“MECIPT-1 Drum memory 2”](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:MECIPT-1_Drum_memory_2.jpg), Wikimedia Commons, 2015, CC BY-SA 4.0 — the magnetic drum, at the Museum of Banat.
