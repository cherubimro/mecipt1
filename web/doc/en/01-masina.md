# MECIPT-1 in brief

**MECIPT-1** — “Mașina Electronică de Calcul a Institutului Politehnic Timișoara” (the electronic computing machine of the Polytechnic Institute of Timișoara) — was put into service in **1961**. It was the first electronic computer built at a university in Romania and the second in the country, after CIFA-1 at Măgurele (Romanian Wikipedia).

## People and years
- The documentation work was started in **1955** by **Iosif Kaufmann** and **Wilhelm Löwenfeld**; the design in **1956**, with Kaufmann as chief designer (Romanian Wikipedia, after Farcaș and Baltac).
- The machine was built in the building of the Piarist High School in Timișoara, then the seat of the Faculty of Electrical Engineering.
- In 1961 **Vasile Baltac**, a fourth-year student, joined the team; in October 1962, the mathematician **Dan Farcaș**. Both later wrote about the machine — their books are the main sources of this emulator.

## The machine
- A first-generation computer with **about 2000 electron tubes** (triodes), over 20,000 capacitors and resistors, 30 km of wire and 100,000 solder joints; it used **about 10 kW** (Romanian Wikipedia, after Farcaș).
- Memory: a **magnetic drum** of **1024 words**, turning **50 times a second**.
- A word had **31 bits**; an instruction 15 bits, so two instructions per word (chapter 2).
- Only addition was hard-wired; the other operations were **microprogrammed** (chapter 4).
- Programs came in on **punched telex tape**, and results came out on an electric **typewriter** (chapter 5).

## What it was used for
Teaching and engineering calculations, including those for the **Vidraru dam** and the **Romexpo** pavilion (dome) in Bucharest (Romanian Wikipedia; Farcaș; Baltac). The code of those programs has not survived; the emulator will only show them as illustrative reconstructions, clearly marked.

## The console today
The control console survived and was restored; its 2011 photograph (Turbojet, Wikimedia Commons) and the 3D model (marius0198, Sketchfab) are the basis of the drawing on this page. Its inscriptions are in Romanian and stay so here; the explanations translate them.

## About this emulator
The emulator runs programs instruction by instruction, with the drum timing, and shows the micro-operations on the left panel. Every choice without a source is marked **CERTAIN**, **DEDUCED** or **GUESSED** — see chapter 7.
